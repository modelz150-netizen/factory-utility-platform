## 1. Constitutional Consistency Review

The five chapters form a consistent constitutional chain:

1. Chapter 1 defines enduring identity and promise.
2. Chapter 2 defines how engineering knowledge earns justified trust.
3. Chapter 3 converts identity and philosophy into durable decision constraints.
4. Chapter 4 defines how Standards derive and maintain enforceable expectations.
5. Chapter 5 defines the constitutional meaning, ownership, and truthful state of quality.

No chapter authorizes convenience, automation, seniority, schedule, or commercial pressure to replace evidence and accountable judgment. Safety, traceability, proportionality, controlled change, and institutional memory remain mutually consistent.

**Result: Pass.**

## 2. Cross-Chapter Duplication Review

Evidence, traceability, proportionality, controlled change, human accountability, and lifecycle quality recur across chapters. The recurrence is controlled and responsibility-specific:

- Chapter 2 treats them as epistemic and engineering philosophy.
- Chapter 3 treats them as universal decision principles.
- Chapter 4 treats them as Standards authority and compliance governance.
- Chapter 5 treats them as quality ownership and status integrity.

No verbatim or procedural duplication requires removal. Future chapters should reference these definitions instead of creating parallel formulations.

**Result: Pass with a watch item for future drafting.**

## 5. Terminology Consistency Review

Core engineering terms are substantively consistent. Two accepted normalization topics remain open:

- `GOV-010`: define Project, Product, Platform, Repository, and Organization usage.
- `GOV-011`: define normative force for `shall`, `must`, `should`, and `may`.

These are future Governance requirements, not defects in approved chapters. Until standardized, reviewers must interpret modal language conservatively and preserve existing meaning.

**Result: Pass with tracked recommendations.**

## 6. Simplification Recommendations

1. Do not consolidate Chapters 2 through 5 merely because key principles recur; their responsibilities are distinct.
2. Future chapters should link or refer to established concepts rather than restating evidence, traceability, proportionality, or controlled-change definitions.
3. Resolve the legacy Charter status through `GOV-016` before the document set grows further.
4. Implement the terminology requirements in their designated future chapter, then apply changes prospectively unless a verified ambiguity requires correction to approved text.
5. Consider a non-authoritative Charter index only after enough chapters exist to justify it; do not add structural material during this milestone.

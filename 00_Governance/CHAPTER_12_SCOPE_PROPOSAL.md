## 1. Purpose

Chapter 12 is proposed to establish the constitutional principles by which planned work moves from intent to accountable completion. The Platform requires a stable basis for prioritization, visibility, review, approval, traceability, handover, and truthful completion that remains valid regardless of delivery method, team structure, cadence, or tool.

This responsibility belongs in the Master Charter because unmanaged workflow can conceal authority, dilute accountability, misrepresent progress, bypass validation, and disconnect delivered change from its approved purpose. Lower-level Governance may define ceremonies, states, boards, templates, and automation, but it cannot decide the enduring duties that make planning and workflow constitutionally trustworthy.

## 2. Constitutional Scope

Chapter 12 shall include only the following enduring Sprint and Workflow Governance principles:

1. **Sprint governance principles** — a Sprint is a bounded planning and review horizon, not a source of authority or a guarantee of completion.
2. **Workflow governance principles** — governed work progresses through explicit, meaningful states with accountable entry, transition, and exit conditions.
3. **Planning accountability** — planned work has an accountable owner, approved purpose, scope boundary, priority basis, dependencies, constraints, and acceptance basis.
4. **Review and approval principles** — review, validation, approval, execution, and acceptance remain distinguishable and are performed by competent authority.
5. **Traceability principles** — material work remains connected to its originating need, governing authority, decisions, evidence, changes, and outcome.
6. **Work visibility** — material status, blockers, uncertainty, scope change, residual work, and decision needs remain truthfully visible to affected authorities.
7. **Governance checkpoints** — consequence-based checkpoints prevent work from silently bypassing required architecture, quality, security, privacy, legal, domain, release, or constitutional review.
8. **Completion accountability** — completion reflects satisfied acceptance, evidence, documentation, known limitations, and ownership of remaining obligations rather than activity or elapsed time.
9. **Handover principles** — transfer of work preserves authority, context, decisions, dependencies, risks, evidence, and unresolved responsibilities.
10. **Scope and change integrity** — material change to intent, consequence, dependency, or acceptance basis remains explicit and re-enters the appropriate Governance decision.
11. **Constitutional workflow integrity** — urgency, automation, organizational rank, or process labels cannot create authority, waive obligations, or convert incomplete work into completed work.
12. **Chapter boundary** — methods and implementation remain assigned to lower-level Governance and future chapters.

## 3. Explicit Exclusions

Chapter 12 shall not include:

- Sprint ceremonies, cadence design, iteration scheduling, backlog refinement meetings, planning meetings, reviews, retrospectives, or daily meetings;
- Agile methodology implementation or organization-specific delivery doctrine;
- Scrum roles, events, artifacts, estimation procedures, velocity, story points, or Definition-of-Done templates;
- Kanban boards, work-in-progress limits, queues, swimlanes, pull systems, or flow metrics;
- task creation, assignment, decomposition, prioritization algorithms, status labels, ticket fields, or task-management operations;
- Git branching, commit conventions, merge strategy, repository permissions, or Git workflow;
- Pull Request creation, review routing, approvals, checks, merge, or branch deletion;
- CI/CD pipelines, build gates, deployment automation, environment promotion, or infrastructure configuration;
- release planning, release approval, deployment, rollback, or Release workflow reserved for Chapter 14;
- operational checklists, meeting agendas, forms, templates, or dashboards;
- product-specific workflow screens, database schemas, integrations, or notifications; or
- tool-specific guidance for GitHub, issue trackers, project boards, chat, documentation, or automation.

These subjects belong to Standards, operating procedures, product requirements, implementation documents, or their approved future Charter chapters.

## 5. Governance Boundaries

| Layer | Chapter 12 responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional principles | Define accountability, visibility, traceability, checkpoints, completion, handover, scope integrity, and non-bypassable workflow duties. | Independent of methodology, cadence, tool, and team design. |
| Standards | Define required work states, evidence, review criteria, exception controls, quality gates, documentation, and compatible lifecycle rules. | Standards implement higher authority and remain responsibility-specific. |
| Operational procedures | Execute planning, scheduling, coordination, meetings, task management, review routing, reporting, and handover. | Procedures remain replaceable and cannot create constitutional approval or waive obligations. |

Chapter 8 continues to own authority and escalation. Chapters 5 and 6 continue to own quality and validation. Chapter 13 will own GitHub Governance, and Chapter 14 will own Release Governance.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Consequence | Proposed control |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicating Chapter 8 | Workflow appears to grant authority or redefine escalation | Reference decision rights and limit Chapter 12 to integrity of work progression. |
| Duplicating Chapters 5 and 6 | Completion becomes a second quality or validation system | Require their evidence without redefining their criteria or authority. |
| Preempting Chapter 13 | GitHub mechanics enter constitutional workflow | Exclude Git and Pull Request implementation explicitly. |
| Preempting Chapter 14 | Readiness and release execution become conflated | Stop at accountable completion and handover; reserve production release authority. |
| Methodology capture | Charter becomes coupled to Agile, Scrum, Kanban, or a cadence | Define method-neutral constitutional principles. |
| Status theater | Visible activity is mistaken for progress or completion | Require evidence, acceptance, blockers, uncertainty, and residual obligations. |
| Excessive process | Governance checkpoints become ceremonial delay | Require consequence-based proportionality and responsible authority. |
| Automated bypass | Tools transition work without valid review or approval | State that automation cannot create authority or waive obligations. |
| Ambiguous handover | Accountability disappears between teams or lifecycle stages | Require explicit transfer of context, risk, evidence, and unresolved duties. |
